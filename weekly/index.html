Read complete facts, analysis and response

The facts

  • A Department of Labor final rule took effect on October 19, removing 20 CFR 652.215 and its requirement that states use state merit-system employees to deliver Wagner-Peyser Employment Service programs. [1]
  • The rule permits, but does not require, states to use alternative staffing models. Labor says states remain responsible for federal performance standards, civil-rights and nondiscrimination requirements, program-integrity safeguards and applicable grant conditions. [1]
  • The rule does not alter existing state collective-bargaining agreements or require conversion of merit staff to at-will employees or contractors. State merit staff must continue making determinations and redeterminations affecting unemployment-compensation eligibility. [1]
  • Labor retained migrant and seasonal farmworker outreach, complaint, monitor-advocate and agricultural job-order safeguards. State Monitor Advocates must remain state employees, though they no longer must be merit staff. [1]
  • Labor said it concluded the Wagner-Peyser Act does not authorize a blanket federal merit-staffing mandate and that flexibility could improve efficiency. The rule also acknowledged insufficient data for a comprehensive cost-benefit analysis, removed a mandatory evaluation of staffing models and said future studies remain possible. [1]
  • An agency implementation notice tells states that choose a different model to submit a WIOA state-plan modification and complete consultation and public-comment steps before implementation. The rule's effect is therefore a new option, not evidence that any state has already outsourced services or improved outcomes. [1]

Significance

The rule changes the federal staffing floor for a nationwide public employment-service system and allows states to reorganize who delivers job-placement and related services. Its practical consequence depends on state choices, labor agreements, plan approvals, oversight and measured service outcomes.

Goalpost / response

Labor says state flexibility will permit more efficient and responsive service delivery without weakening federal accountability or worker protections. State-plan modifications, staffing conversions, procurement terms, worker transitions, service access, processing times, job-placement outcomes, complaints, farmworker compliance, costs and federal monitoring are the tests.

Maybe / Therefore

Maybe alternative staffing lets states integrate services and direct more resources to job seekers, or it may reduce expertise, independence and accountability without the evaluation the rule removed. Therefore the federal merit-staffing mandate has ended and states gained an option—not a requirement to privatize, proof that any state has changed models, or evidence of better or worse outcomes.

Read complete facts, analysis and response

The facts

  • In precedential Matter of L. Ramirez, the Board of Immigration Appeals held that a person with a prima facie approvable U visa petition has no presumption in favor of a continuance of removal proceedings, overruling Matter of Sanchez Sosa (2012). [1]
  • The Board directed immigration judges to apply the general Matter of L-A-B-R- balancing test, focusing on the likelihood and material effect of collateral relief while also weighing diligence, DHS's position, requested length and case history. [1]
  • The decision says good cause does not exist when visa availability is too remote and reaffirms that respondents are not entitled to delay completion of removal proceedings pending visa availability. It attributes long U visa waits to the statutory annual cap and demand exceeding supply. [1]
  • The Board acknowledged that the respondent appeared prima facie eligible and that approval would materially affect removability, but upheld denial after an open-ended request, years of delay, no evidence of adjudication and DHS opposition. [1]
  • The decision governs continuance analysis in immigration adjudication; it does not deny every U visa petition, eliminate case-specific balancing or itself decide USCIS's underlying petition. [1]

Significance

Removing the U visa-specific presumption can accelerate removal proceedings for crime victims whose petitions remain pending for years, shifting greater weight to timing, DHS opposition and procedural history while retaining individualized balancing.

Goalpost / response

The Board emphasizes prompt enforcement and a uniform good-cause standard; U visa applicants rely on continuances so collateral protection can be adjudicated before removal. Immigration-judge application rates, continuance grants and denials, removals before USCIS action, appellate review and U visa processing times are the tests.

Maybe / Therefore
