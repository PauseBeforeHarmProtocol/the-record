  • The Texas action waives the state-plan low-emission diesel standard and federal ultra-low-sulfur diesel requirement through December 3 for certain older highway vehicles and nonroad equipment originally certified for fuel containing up to 500 parts per million sulfur.   • The Arizona action waives federal enforcement of the state's Cleaner Burning Gasoline boutique-fuel program through December 6 and allows specified gasoline containing 9% to 15% ethanol to use older ASTM volatility limits. Other federal, state and local fuel requirements remain applicable unless separately waived. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • The Alaska action runs November 14 through December 3 only above the Arctic Circle and permits high-sulfur diesel in pre-1994 highway engines and older nonroad equipment originally certified for it. Equipment requiring ultra-low-sulfur diesel remains subject to that standard. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • EPA said Middle East disruptions, high refinery utilization and below-average fuel inventories created extreme and unusual supply circumstances and that temporary flexibility was in the public interest. The letters do not measure how much fuel the waivers added, the price effect, actual use of higher-sulfur fuel or resulting emissions and equipment impacts. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Significance

The renewals use emergency authority to relax emissions-related fuel standards across three geographically distinct markets during a prolonged supply response. Their narrow duration and equipment limits reduce but do not eliminate environmental, misfueling and accountability questions; operational effects must be measured rather than inferred from the waivers themselves.

Goalpost / response

EPA says the waivers widen available supply and reduce pressure on constrained gasoline and diesel markets while protecting newer emissions-control equipment. The tests are gallons produced or distributed under each waiver, verified eligible equipment use and misfueling incidents, regional inventory and price changes, air-quality and emissions data, equipment damage, extensions and restoration of ordinary standards at expiration.
