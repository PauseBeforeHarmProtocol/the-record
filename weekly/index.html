The facts

  • The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation proposed amendments to its brokered-deposit regulations to implement section 901 of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act. Comments are due January 19, 2027; the notice is a proposal, not a final rule. [1]
  • The proposal would allow an eligible insured depository institution with less than $10 billion in assets to treat qualifying custodial deposits up to 20 percent of total liabilities as non-brokered, subject to statutory eligibility and interest-rate restrictions. [1]
  • A bank, qualifying entity or other party in the placement arrangement would have to submit a one-time notice effective upon filing, certify it annually and update material changes. The FDIC would publish a list of qualifying notices. [1]
  • The FDIC estimates 4,031 institutions could meet the proposed eligibility definition and that as many as 1,629 could be immediately affected if the rule were finalized. It estimates about $450 in labor cost for an initial notice and $112 for annual certification, while saying some deposit-insurance assessments could fall but aggregate effects are likely small. [1]

Significance

The proposal could change how up to 1,629 banks classify custodial funding and how some institutions are assessed for deposit insurance. The statutory exception already exists, but the proposed definitions, notice system and supervisory treatment would shape its operation.

Goalpost / response

The FDIC says the proposal would clarify the statutory exception, reduce an asset-threshold cliff and make qualifying arrangements transparent. The tests are the final text, treatment of comments, number and identity of notice filers, reclassification of deposits, insurance-assessment effects, funding and liquidity changes, compliance costs, supervisory outcomes and any effect on deposit-insurance risk.

Maybe / Therefore

Maybe the proposal will give community banks clearer access to stable custodial deposits with limited compliance costs, or reclassification could change funding incentives and risk in ways the present estimates do not capture. Therefore the FDIC opened a rulemaking and supplied estimates; it has not finalized the definitions, approved a notice or measured bank, depositor or insurance-fund outcomes.

Significance

The California dismissal and Alaska mootness request close or seek to close the administration's two most consequential post-election certification challenges without a merits victory. DOJ is asking the Ninth Circuit to erase the Alaska standing dismissal rather than affirm it, so the litigation's practical threat has receded while the precedential and sanctions questions remain unresolved.

Goalpost / response

DOJ says election timing, commingled ballots and the completed count made the requested relief unavailable while continuing to defend its underlying legal theory and opposing sanctions. The tests are the Ninth Circuit's disposition, whether the district dismissal is vacated or affirmed, any sanctions decision, certification, independently supported follow-on enforcement and verified election effects.

Maybe / Therefore

Maybe DOJ's Alaska request reflects the genuine impossibility of reconstructing voter intent after ballots were counted, or the election result and adverse standing ruling also made further merits litigation untenable. Therefore DOJ asked for mootness vacatur and remand; the Ninth Circuit had not granted that request, affirmed the standing ruling, imposed sanctions or adjudicated the government's voter-confusion allegations by cutoff.

Significance

The complaint turns previously reported troop and family concerns into a formal inspector-general and congressional accountability request with preservation implications. It directly tests the administration's prior claim that the post was fortified, while Trump's response broadens the defense to theater-wide outcomes without resolving site-specific warnings, protection or medical readiness.

Goalpost / response

Trump says U.S. defenses have been historically effective and cites broad weapon and fatality figures. The tests are the complaint and preserved records, an independent inspector-general or congressional inquiry, the Army's completed but unreleased review, force-protection assessments, warning and air-defense logs, medical-preparation evidence, casualty findings and any corrective or disciplinary action—not aggregate presidential claims alone.

Maybe / Therefore

Maybe the complaint will show commanders made reasonable choices under uncertain threat conditions, or it may substantiate preventable force-protection and medical-preparation failures. Therefore a wounded officer has filed a formal request for independent review and Trump has published a defense of overall performance—but neither the allegations nor his numerical claims are adjudicated findings about Port Shuaiba.
