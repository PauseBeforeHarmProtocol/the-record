  • CMS issued State Medicaid Director Letter 26-006 offering every state an optional hold-harmless period through June 30, 2027, for the new Medicaid community-engagement requirement. States may choose a shorter period or decline the option. [1]

Significance

The November 12 statement reverses the administration's latest public suggestion that Doha contacts could produce a settlement and places the unresolved conflict back on a pressure-without-agreement footing. Because no order or operational change was published, it changes declared policy and near-term diplomatic risk without proving negotiations ended or hostilities expanded.

Goalpost / response

Trump now says no deal is needed and invokes U.S. leverage over the strait; earlier, Vance described mediated talks as good and a settlement as possible. The tests are a written negotiating directive, confirmed cancellation or continuation of talks, a ceasefire or strike order, force or sanctions changes, a public Iranian response and verified operational effects—not a social-media declaration alone.

Maybe / Therefore

Maybe the statement is a bargaining posture while back-channel contacts continue, or it may signal that the administration has abandoned the latest diplomatic effort while maintaining an open-ended blockade. Therefore the archive records a material reversal in Trump's announced position and an asserted redirection of Vance—not a verified end to negotiations, formal delegation, U.S. ownership of Hormuz, renewed strike order or settlement.

  • The plaintiffs allege DHS could not lawfully invoke section 102 of the Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act because the 517-mile Big Bend sector is not an area of ‘high illegal entry.’ Their complaint cites CBP statistics showing the sector accounted for about 1.16% of Southwest-border apprehensions in fiscal years 2021–2025. Those figures and legal conclusions are pleaded allegations, not judicial findings. [1]

  • Associated Press and the plaintiffs reported that the court found the plaintiffs likely to succeed on at least one claim, likely to face irreparable harm without relief, and favored by the equities and public interest.   • On November 9, the Supreme Court granted the government’s application in Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church, No. 26A560, and stayed the Western District of Texas preliminary injunction. The stay remains in effect through the timely certiorari process: it ends automatically if certiorari is denied and, if review is granted, when the Court sends down its judgment. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson would have denied the application. [1]
  • The per curiam opinion said the government is exceedingly likely to show that the district court lacked jurisdiction because respondents’ claim is statutory rather than constitutional. The Court also relied on a CBP declaration estimating nearly $500,000 in unrecoverable costs per day while construction was enjoined and concluded that Congress’s balance in IIRIRA section 102(c) favored expeditious construction over the environmental and recreational harms alleged. [1]
  • The stay allows construction covered by the injunction to resume while the certiorari process continues. It does not finally resolve the merits, validate every project or waiver, decide the separate District of Columbia litigation, or establish whether the government will complete particular work before later review. [1]

Significance

The Supreme Court has changed the operative posture from a lower-court construction pause to a temporary stay that permits covered work while the government pursues certiorari. The Court’s jurisdiction analysis is consequential but remains part of an emergency stay decision, not a final merits judgment.

Goalpost / response

The administration says statutory waiver authority, border-security needs and unrecoverable costs justify continued construction. Respondents argue the projects exceed statutory authority and threaten religious, environmental, recreational and property interests. The tests are the certiorari petition and disposition, any merits ruling, project-level records, verified construction activity and final relief in both lawsuits.

Maybe / Therefore

Maybe the jurisdiction ruling will lead to dismissal and uninterrupted construction, or later merits and project-specific litigation may still limit particular work. Therefore the record establishes a Supreme Court stay allowing covered construction to resume during the certiorari process—not a final validation of every Big Bend project or final rejection of all plaintiffs’ claims.

The facts

  • The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on September 11 that the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers rose 0.4% on a seasonally adjusted basis in August after a 0.1% July increase. The all-items index was 3.4% higher than a year earlier, unchanged from July's annual rate. [1]
  • Gasoline rose 3.9% during August and accounted for more than one-third of the monthly all-items increase. Over 12 months, gasoline rose 27.4%, fuel oil 52.0% and the overall energy index 16.3%. [1]
  • Food rose 0.1% during August and 2.7% over 12 months; food at home was unchanged during the month and 2.2% higher annually. Shelter rose 0.3% during August. [1]
  • The index excluding food and energy rose 0.3% during August, its largest monthly rise since April, and 2.4% over 12 months. Reuters reported that financial markets subsequently priced a roughly 91% chance of a Federal Reserve rate increase the following week; that market estimate is not an enacted rate decision. [1] [2]
  • BLS reported on October 14 that the Consumer Price Index rose 0.6% on a seasonally adjusted basis in September after the 0.4% August increase. The all-items index was 3.7% higher than a year earlier, up from 3.4% in August. [1]
  • Gasoline rose 9.1% in September and accounted for more than half of the monthly all-items increase. The broader energy index rose 5.2% during the month and 20.4% over 12 months; food was unchanged during the month and food at home fell 0.1%. [1]
  • The index excluding food and energy rose 0.3% during September and 2.6% over 12 months, compared with 2.4% annual core inflation in August. Reuters reported market-implied odds of an October Federal Reserve rate increase moved to about 52% from 22%; that probability is not an enacted rate decision. [1] [2]
  • BLS reported on November 10 that the Consumer Price Index rose 0.4% on a seasonally adjusted basis in October after September's 0.6% increase. Shelter rose 0.3% and accounted for more than 30% of the monthly all-items increase; food rose 0.5%, energy rose 0.7% and gasoline rose 1.0%. [1]
  • The index excluding food and energy rose 0.3% in October, the same monthly increase as in September. Reuters reported the headline and core increases exceeded economists' median forecasts of 0.3% and 0.2%, respectively; forecasts and market reactions are context, not official inflation measures. [1] [2]
  • BLS did not publish 12-month October indexes because the 2025 government shutdown prevented collection of October 2025 consumer-price data. The missing comparison means no official October year-over-year CPI rate was available; it does not mean annual inflation was zero or unchanged. [1]
  • Reuters reported financial markets priced roughly an 89% chance of a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate increase in December after the release. That probability is not an enacted monetary-policy decision, and neither BLS nor the CPI release assigns the monthly change to a single administration policy, war, tariff or supply factor. [1]

Significance

The October release shows a third consecutive month of at least 0.4% headline CPI growth and continued 0.3% core growth, with increases across shelter, food and energy. The unavailable annual comparison is a material data limitation; monthly indexes still measure price change but do not establish one political cause or every household's experience.

Administration officials have argued for lower prices and interest rates, while the Federal Reserve targets 2% inflation using a different index. Maybe the October monthly increase fades as energy costs ease, or persistent shelter, food and core increases may keep inflation pressure elevated. Therefore October shows measured 0.4% headline and 0.3% core CPI growth, with no official 12-month rate because the comparison base is missing—not zero annual inflation, proof of one cause or a completed Federal Reserve response.
