The facts

  • Trump wrote on September 26 that he had approved new fuel-economy standards. Reuters reported that Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the standards would be unveiled Monday, September 28, and that DOT officials described Monday as the expected finalization date. [1] [2]
  • The operative text available at cutoff remained NHTSA's December 2025 notice of proposed rulemaking for passenger cars and light trucks in model years 2022 through 2031. That proposal projected a roughly 34.5-mile-per-gallon fleetwide average for model year 2031, compared with 50.4 mpg under the prior standards, and proposed ending intermanufacturer credit trading beginning in model year 2028. [1]
  • Reuters reported DOT's own proposal estimates: about $930 less in average upfront vehicle cost, roughly 100 billion additional gallons of fuel consumed through 2050, about $185 billion in additional fuel spending and approximately 5% more carbon-dioxide emissions. These are modeled projections, not measured outcomes of a final rule. [1]
  • The administration says lower standards will improve consumer choice, affordability and vehicle-fleet renewal. Former Transportation Secretary Pete Buttigieg responded that lower standards would make drivers pay more at the pump and weaken U.S. clean-technology competitiveness. No final regulatory text, signature, Federal Register publication, effective date or implementation evidence was located by the cutoff. [1] [2]

Significance

The announcement advances a major vehicle-cost, fuel-use and climate policy from proposal toward final action, but the legal and practical change depends on the final instrument. The proposal's own modeling identifies a tradeoff between lower upfront prices and substantially higher lifetime fuel use, spending and emissions.

Goalpost / response

The administration frames the rollback as affordability, safety and consumer choice rather than an electric-vehicle mandate. The strongest response is that lower purchase prices may be offset by higher fuel costs and emissions. The final text, regulatory analysis, publication and effective dates, manufacturer compliance plans, litigation, vehicle prices, fuel consumption and emissions are the tests.

Maybe / Therefore

Maybe the final rule will materially lower vehicle purchase prices and let manufacturers better match consumer demand, or higher lifetime fuel spending and weaker efficiency incentives may outweigh those gains. Therefore the record treats September 26 as an announced finalization following an existing proposal—not as a published, effective or implemented final rule, and not as proof of any projected savings, fuel use or emissions outcome.

The facts

  • The White House announced on September 25 that Trump was using a pocket rescission for eleven unspent funding pools whose listed amounts total $810 million. [1] [2]
  • The package includes $567 million in HHS services for refugees, asylees and other noncitizens, plus $243 million across DHS immigrant programs, migrant-student education, the Justice Department's Community Relations Service, Treasury conservation debt relief, minority-business development, international education, HHS research and minority health, and HUD housing counseling. [1] [2]
  • AP reported that the administration announced the proposal with five days remaining in the fiscal year and the House out of session through the election. The Impoundment Control Act otherwise permits withholding proposed rescissions for 45 days of continuous congressional session while Congress considers whether to approve them. [1] [2]
  • The administration calls the programs wasteful, unnecessary or discriminatory. Republican Senate Appropriations Chair Susan Collins called the delay and maneuver illegal, and GAO's published position is that the Act does not permit funds to be withheld through expiration. Congress had not approved this package and no court had ruled on its legality by cutoff. [1] [2] [3]

Significance

The package uses fiscal-year timing to try to make $810 million in domestic appropriations expire before Congress can complete the review process, directly testing the constitutional allocation of spending power and immediately placing services and grants at risk.

Goalpost / response

The administration says reduced border crossings, duplication and ideological grantmaking make the money unnecessary or harmful. The strongest response is that the executive delayed the request to defeat Congress's statutory review. The special message, agency obligation records, congressional action, GAO findings, litigation and any documented program or fiscal effects are the tests.

Maybe / Therefore

Maybe much of the money would otherwise remain unobligated or fund programs that the administration can justify ending, or the late withholding may unlawfully prevent agencies from carrying out enacted appropriations. Therefore the record establishes an announced and operative late-year rescission attempt—not congressional approval, settled legality, realized savings or measured effects on beneficiaries.
